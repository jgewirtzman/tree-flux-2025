This dataset contains stem methane (CH4) and carbon dioxide (CO2) fluxes of 60 trees at two contrasting sites in the Prospect Hill tract of Harvard Forest, Petersham, Massachusetts, USA, measured from June 2023 to October 2025, together with the raw analyzer records, field logs and processing decisions from which the fluxes were calculated, hourly environmental drivers, sonic and electrical resistance tomography of internal wood condition, and the tree and stand data that describe the study design.

Fluxes were measured with permanent collars 1 m above the base of ten trees of each of four species: black gum (Nyssa sylvatica) at Black Gum Swamp, a groundwater-fed peat wetland; red oak (Quercus rubra) in upland forest in the footprint of the Environmental Measurement Station (EMS) eddy covariance tower; and red maple (Acer rubrum) and eastern hemlock (Tsuga canadensis) at both sites. Closed-chamber measurements (1,637 in the final dataset) were made with a Los Gatos Research GLA131 analyzer (June 2023-March 2025) and a LI-COR LI-7810 analyzer (April-October 2025), approximately weekly in summer, biweekly in spring and fall and monthly in winter. The record includes a severe drought in 2025.

All fluxes were calculated from the raw 1-Hz records with one reproducible procedure (goFlux linear and Hutchinson-Mosier fits), and every measurement carries a minimum detectable flux, detection flags and quality-control flags (goFlux). Fluxes below the detection limit are retained. Every cleaning rule and the number of records it affected are listed in a processing log, and the analysis code that rebuilds every file in this package from the raw records is archived separately (see Methods).

These data support Gewirtzman et al., "Contrasting controls on tree methane emissions in upland and wetland forests" (Journal of Geophysical Research: Biogeosciences, submitted). Tomography methods and the per-tree decay classification follow Thompson et al. (2026, bioRxiv, https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.10.730433).
