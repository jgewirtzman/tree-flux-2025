# Study design

The study was conducted in the Prospect Hill tract of Harvard Forest, Petersham, Massachusetts, USA, at two sites about 1 km apart. The upland site lies in the footprint of the Environmental Measurement Station (EMS) eddy covariance tower (AmeriFlux US-Ha1), where trees were chosen from nine of the 40 EMS inventory plots and the surrounding ForestGEO plot. The wetland site is Black Gum Swamp (BGS), a groundwater-fed peat basin with hummock-hollow microtopography. Ten trees of each species were selected at each site where the species occurs: Nyssa sylvatica (bg) at BGS, Quercus rubra (ro) at EMS, and Acer rubrum (rm) and Tsuga canadensis (hem) at both sites (60 trees). Trees were selected on size alone (second and third quartiles of each species' stand DBH distribution), without regard to external signs of decay. Trees are identified by their study tag numbers; tree locations were surveyed with GPS (wetland April 2025, upland January 2026; trees.csv and the GeoJSON files). Wetland trees were classified as standing on a hummock, in a hollow, or on the transition.

# Stem flux measurements

Permanent collars of PVC pipe (inner diameter 10.16 cm; depth 3.8-5.1 cm, measured at four interior points per collar) were attached 1 m above the base of each tree in May 2023 with non-VOC silicone sealant. Curved collars were shaped to the stem; flat collars were used on flatter stems. For each measurement a cap lined with closed-cell foam closed the collar and gas circulated between chamber and analyzer in a closed loop for at least 3 minutes. The seal was tested before each measurement by breathing around the collar-cap junction while watching CO2 in real time; leaking collars were resealed and measured again after the sealant cured.

Measurements from June 2023 to March 2025 were made with a Los Gatos Research Microportable Greenhouse Gas Analyzer (GLA131; recorded as LGR1/LGR3, "LGR/UGGA"), and from April to October 2025 with a LI-COR LI-7810 trace gas analyzer, both at 1 Hz. Sampling was approximately weekly in summer, biweekly in spring and fall, and monthly in winter. Start and end times of each closure were recorded on field datasheets (LGR period) or as tagged remarks in the analyzer record (LI-7810 period). measurements.csv lists the 1,872 measurements of the study, each with its tree, analyzer, chamber closure and opening times on the analyzer clock (after the field team's timing corrections), sampling time on the local clock, and the air temperature and pressure at the time of measurement (Fisher Meteorological Station).

# Flux calculation and quality control

All fluxes were calculated from the raw 1-Hz analyzer records (analyzer_raw_lgr.zip, analyzer_raw_li7810.zip) with one procedure (scripts/2_flux/ in the analysis code). The first 20 s after chamber closure were discarded (deadband; deadband_s is 0 where the start of the record was set on inspection), and each window ended at the recorded chamber opening or earlier where a sustained fall in CO2 showed the chamber had been opened. Linear and Hutchinson-Mosier models were fitted with the goFlux R package (Rheault et al. 2024, https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.06393) and the better estimate selected with goFlux best.flux (curvature bounded following Hüppi et al. 2018). Flux = slope x n / A, where n is the moles of gas in the closed system (ideal gas law with air temperature and pressure from the Fisher Meteorological Station) and A the enclosed bark area (81.07 cm2). The system volume is the measured collar volume plus cap and tubing (chamber_volumes.csv) plus 0.028 L of analyzer internal volume. Every measurement was fitted from its raw record.

For each measurement a minimum detectable flux was calculated as MDF = 1.96 sigma / t x n / A (1.96 is a benchmark multiplier on concentration noise, not a calibrated 95 % detection test; Cowan et al. 2025, https://doi.org/10.1111/ejss.70104), where t is the closure length and sigma the analyzer precision on that day, estimated for each analyzer and field day from the closure windows as the median, over that day's closures, of the MAD-based standard deviation of second differences of the concentration divided by the square root of 6 (goFlux empirical.prec; goFlux version 0.5.0.9002 with additions, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23256675). Fluxes below the MDF are retained and flagged (CH4_below_MDF, CH4_det_class). goFlux quality screens (qc_*) and automated checks of every CO2 and CH4 trace (trace_*) are provided as flags; they do not remove data. All settings are in flux_processing_settings.json. The two analyzers were not run side by side, so an offset between them cannot be separated from differences between 2023-2024 and 2025.

Times: all times are local. sample_time_local, closure_start and closure_end are on the local wall clock (America/New_York; closure times on the analyzer clock); sample_hour_est and the datetime of environment_hourly.csv are on Eastern Standard Time (UTC-5, no daylight saving), used to join the flux measurements to the hourly environmental data.

# Environmental drivers

environment_hourly.csv gives hourly values (EST, UTC-5) from January 2023 to August 2026 (coverage varies by variable; see the dictionary), compiled from public datasets:

- Harvard Forest Fisher Meteorological Station, HF001 (Boose and VanScoy, https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/7a7ffd2eaa2ea9965d701998d4e2b1f5): air temperature, relative humidity, pressure, precipitation, PAR, net and global solar radiation, soil temperature at 10 cm; vapour pressure deficit calculated from air temperature and relative humidity (plantecophys R package).
- Prospect Hill Hydrological Stations, HF070 (Boose and VanScoy, https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/5811c1e815ca6a07859b99fbe6d07a30): water level (stage) at Black Gum Swamp and Beaver Swamp.
- AmeriFlux BASE US-Ha1, Harvard Forest EMS Tower, Ver. 28-5 (Munger et al., https://doi.org/10.17190/AMF/1246059) and US-Ha2, Harvard Forest Hemlock Site, Ver. 17-5 (Hadley and Munger, https://doi.org/10.17190/AMF/1246060), accessed 30 September 2026: CO2 flux and storage, latent and sensible heat flux, friction velocity, CO2 mole fraction (US-Ha2), soil temperature and soil water content. Funding for the AmeriFlux data service was provided by the U.S. Department of Energy Office of Science.
- NEON, Harvard Forest site HARV, RELEASE-2026 and provisional data for July-December 2025, accessed 30 September 2026: soil water content (DP1.00094.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/h349-2y77), eddy-covariance bundle (DP4.00200.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/xymh-2v16: CO2 flux and storage, friction velocity, CO2 and CH4 mole fractions), IR canopy temperature (DP1.00005.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/aqsx-9j37), throughfall (DP1.00046.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/43q7-9670) and soil heat flux (DP1.00040.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/afws-4652). NEON provisional data (July-December 2025) have no DOI; the values derived from them are archived in this package. This material is based in part upon work supported by the National Ecological Observatory Network (NEON), a program sponsored by the U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) and operated under cooperative agreement by Battelle.
- PhenoCam Network, camera harvardems2 (deciduous broadleaf ROI DB_1000): daily green chromatic coordinate (90th percentile) and NDVI (90th percentile), repeated for each hour of the day.

Series measured at 15 or 30 min were averaged to the hour (precipitation summed). Soil water content and soil temperature from multi-sensor networks (NEON soil plots; AmeriFlux tower profiles) are baseline-adjusted means: each sensor's departure from its own median in 2024 was averaged across sensors and added to the 2024 median of all sensors, so that sensor gaps do not shift the mean. The column definitions (environment_hourly_dictionary.csv) give the source and processing of every variable.

# Tomography and decay classes

Sonic tomography (SoT) and electrical resistance tomography (ERT) were measured once on each tree at breast height (1.37 m) between 2 July and 2 August 2024 with the PiCUS system (Argus Electronic GmbH; SoT module and TreeTronic 3 for ERT). SoT structural loss is the percentage of the cross-section in non-brown (low-velocity) classes. Eight resistivity metrics were extracted from each ERT image with the ERT image analysis application of the Tree-Tomography repository (https://github.com/graceethompson/Tree-Tomography). The tomography tables and tomograms are published as a separate Harvard Forest dataset (tomography of upland and wetland trees at Harvard Forest 2024); this dataset holds only the decay class derived from them. Decay classes (tomography_classes.csv) follow Thompson et al. (2026): structural loss is more than 1 % of the SoT cross-section in non-brown classes; anomalous moisture is a value above the study-set mean on the first principal component of the eight ERT metrics, each standardized within species; class I no decay (neither), II incipient decay (moisture anomaly only), III active decay (both), IV cavity (structural loss only). Full procedures and validation against gravimetric moisture are in Thompson et al. (2026).

# Stand survey

BGS_VRP_2025.csv is a variable-radius (prism) survey of Black Gum Swamp made in June 2025: 40 systematic points with a basal area factor of 10 ft2/acre (2.296 m2/ha), recording each tallied stem's species, DBH and status. Black_Gum_Swamp.kmz is the outline of the swamp (used for the site map).
