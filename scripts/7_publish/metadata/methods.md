# Study design

The study was conducted in the Prospect Hill tract of Harvard Forest, Petersham, Massachusetts, USA, at two sites about 1 km apart. The upland site lies in the footprint of the Environmental Measurement Station (EMS) eddy covariance tower (AmeriFlux US-Ha1), where trees were chosen from nine of the 40 EMS inventory plots and the surrounding ForestGEO plot. The wetland site is Black Gum Swamp (BGS), a groundwater-fed peat basin with hummock-hollow microtopography. Ten trees of each species were selected at each site where the species occurs: Nyssa sylvatica (bg) at BGS, Quercus rubra (ro) at EMS, and Acer rubrum (rm) and Tsuga canadensis (hem) at both sites (60 trees). Trees were selected on size alone (second and third quartiles of each species' stand DBH distribution), without regard to external signs of decay. Tree tags are ForestGEO tags; tree locations were surveyed with GPS (wetland April 2025, upland January 2026; trees.csv and the GeoJSON files). Wetland trees were classified as standing on a hummock, in a hollow, or on the transition.

# Stem flux measurements

Permanent collars of PVC pipe (inner diameter 10.16 cm; depth 3.8-5.1 cm, measured at four interior points per collar) were attached 1 m above the base of each tree in May 2023 with non-VOC silicone sealant. Curved collars were shaped to the stem; flat collars were used on flatter stems. For each measurement a cap lined with closed-cell foam closed the collar and gas circulated between chamber and analyzer in a closed loop for at least 3 minutes. The seal was tested before each measurement by breathing around the collar-cap junction while watching CO2 in real time; leaking collars were resealed and measured again after the sealant cured.

Measurements from June 2023 to March 2025 were made with a Los Gatos Research Microportable Greenhouse Gas Analyzer (GLA131; recorded as LGR1/LGR3, "LGR/UGGA"), and from April to October 2025 with a LI-COR LI-7810 trace gas analyzer, both at 1 Hz. Sampling was approximately weekly in summer, biweekly in spring and fall, and monthly in winter. Start and end times of each closure were recorded on field datasheets (field_logs.zip; LGR period) or as tagged remarks in the analyzer record (LI-7810 period).

# Flux calculation and quality control

All fluxes were calculated from the raw 1-Hz analyzer records (analyzer_raw_lgr.zip, analyzer_raw_li7810.zip) with one procedure (scripts/2_flux/ in the analysis code). Closures were assembled from the field logs and analyzer remarks, applying the field team's timing corrections and removing unusable, duplicate, aborted and superseded entries. The first 20 s after chamber closure were discarded (deadband), and each window ended at the recorded end time or earlier where a sustained fall in CO2 showed the chamber had been opened. Linear and Hutchinson-Mosier models were fitted with the goFlux R package (Rheault et al. 2024, https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.06393) and the better estimate selected with goFlux best.flux (curvature bounded following Hüppi et al. 2018). Flux = slope x n / A, where n is the moles of gas in the closed system (ideal gas law with air temperature and pressure from the Fisher Meteorological Station) and A the enclosed bark area (81.07 cm2). The system volume is the measured collar volume plus cap and tubing (chamber_volumes.csv) plus 0.028 L of analyzer internal volume. Ninety-nine measurements without an archived 1-Hz record (October 2025, one day in May 2024 and six closures in September 2023) keep the linear flux of the earlier processing (HF_2023-2025_tree_flux_v1.csv); they are identified by flux_source and fitted.

For each measurement a minimum detectable flux was calculated as MDF = 1.96 sigma / t x n / A (95 %, two-sided), where t is the closure length and sigma the analyzer precision on that day, estimated as the median absolute deviation of first differences over that day's full raw record divided by the square root of 2 (fluxqc R package, https://github.com/jgewirtzman/fluxqc). Fluxes below the MDF are retained and flagged (CH4_below_MDF, CH4_det_class). fluxqc physical screens (qc_*) and automated checks of every CO2 and CH4 trace (trace_*) are provided as flags; they do not remove data. The 55 measurements with the clearest problems were inspected individually (manual_windows.csv): 50 were kept as fitted, two were refitted over a manually chosen window, and three were excluded because of a concentration spike at the start of the record. Every cleaning rule and the number of records it affected are listed in flux_processing_log.csv, and all settings in flux_processing_settings.json. The two analyzers were not run side by side, so an offset between them cannot be separated from differences between 2023-2024 and 2025.

Times: all times are local. sample_time_local and datetime_posx are on the local wall clock (America/New_York); sample_hour_est and the datetime of environment_hourly.csv are on Eastern Standard Time (UTC-5, no daylight saving), used to join the flux measurements to the hourly environmental data. In the earlier dataset (HF_2023-2025_tree_flux_v1.csv), datetime_posx keeps the format of the original file (YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ssZ); its trailing Z does not denote UTC.

# Environmental drivers

environment_hourly.csv gives hourly values (EST, UTC-5) from January 2023 to August 2026 (coverage varies by variable; see the dictionary), compiled from public datasets:

- Harvard Forest Fisher Meteorological Station, HF001 (Boose and VanScoy, https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/7a7ffd2eaa2ea9965d701998d4e2b1f5): air temperature, relative humidity, pressure, precipitation, PAR, net and global solar radiation, soil temperature at 10 cm; vapour pressure deficit calculated from air temperature and relative humidity (plantecophys R package).
- Prospect Hill Hydrological Stations, HF070 (Boose and VanScoy, https://doi.org/10.6073/pasta/5811c1e815ca6a07859b99fbe6d07a30): water level (stage) at Black Gum Swamp and Beaver Swamp.
- AmeriFlux BASE US-Ha1, Harvard Forest EMS Tower, Ver. 28-5 (Munger et al., https://doi.org/10.17190/AMF/1246059) and US-Ha2, Harvard Forest Hemlock Site, Ver. 17-5 (Hadley and Munger, https://doi.org/10.17190/AMF/1246060), accessed 30 September 2026: CO2 flux and storage, latent and sensible heat flux, friction velocity, CO2 mole fraction (US-Ha2), soil temperature and soil water content. Funding for the AmeriFlux data service was provided by the U.S. Department of Energy Office of Science.
- NEON, Harvard Forest site HARV, RELEASE-2026 and provisional data for July-December 2025, accessed 30 September 2026: soil water content (DP1.00094.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/h349-2y77), eddy-covariance bundle (DP4.00200.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/xymh-2v16: CO2 flux and storage, friction velocity, CO2 and CH4 mole fractions), IR canopy temperature (DP1.00005.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/aqsx-9j37), throughfall (DP1.00046.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/43q7-9670) and soil heat flux (DP1.00040.001, https://doi.org/10.48443/afws-4652). NEON provisional data (July-December 2025) have no DOI; the values derived from them are archived in this package. This material is based in part upon work supported by the National Ecological Observatory Network (NEON), a program sponsored by the U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) and operated under cooperative agreement by Battelle.
- PhenoCam Network, camera harvardems2 (deciduous broadleaf ROI DB_1000): daily green chromatic coordinate (90th percentile) and NDVI (90th percentile), repeated for each hour of the day.

Series measured at 15 or 30 min were averaged to the hour (precipitation summed). Soil water content and soil temperature from multi-sensor networks (NEON soil plots; AmeriFlux tower profiles) are baseline-adjusted means: each sensor's departure from its own median in 2024 was averaged across sensors and added to the 2024 median of all sensors, so that sensor gaps do not shift the mean. The column definitions (environment_hourly_dictionary.csv) give the source and processing of every variable.

# Tomography and decay classes

Sonic tomography (SoT) and electrical resistance tomography (ERT) were measured once on each tree at breast height (1.37 m) in July-August 2024 (one tree in February 2024) with the PiCUS system (Argus Electronic GmbH; SoT module and TreeTronic 3 for ERT). SoT structural loss is the percentage of the cross-section in non-brown (low-velocity) classes (Tree_ID_info.csv; sot columns of tomography_results_compiled.csv). Eight resistivity metrics were extracted from each ERT image with the ERT image analysis application of the Tree-Tomography repository (https://github.com/graceethompson/Tree-Tomography; ERT_application_results.csv, also in tomography_results_compiled.csv). The tomograms are in tomography_images.zip (ERTs_Absolute: ERT images; CH4_PITs: SoT images; file names are tree_date, e.g. 412_22vii24 = tree 412, 22 July 2024). Decay classes (tomography_classes.csv) follow Thompson et al. (2026): structural loss is more than 1 % of the SoT cross-section in non-brown classes; anomalous moisture is a value above the study-set mean on the first principal component of the eight ERT metrics, each standardized within species; class I no decay (neither), II incipient decay (moisture anomaly only), III active decay (both), IV cavity (structural loss only). Full procedures and validation against gravimetric moisture are in Thompson et al. (2026).

# Stand survey

BGS_VRP_2025.csv is a variable-radius (prism) survey of Black Gum Swamp made in June 2025: 40 systematic points with a basal area factor of 10 ft2/acre (2.296 m2/ha), recording each tallied stem's species, DBH and status. Black_Gum_Swamp.kmz is the outline of the swamp (used for the site map).

# Earlier processing

HF_2023-2025_tree_flux_v1.csv is the earlier processed flux dataset (linear fits over the logged windows; the version described in the preprint of this study). It is included because the current processing reads its curated tree IDs and meteorology, and keeps its fluxes for the 99 measurements without a raw record. Its fluxes are superseded by stem_ch4_flux.csv for all other measurements.
